40 is the new 80 BY JODY DUNCAN 1 / 3 This issue marks the 40th anniversary of Cinefex — and, in the realm of magazine publishing, 40 years makes Cinefex a very old lady, indeed. Well - resourced mainstream publications such as Life and Look didn’t make it to 40. Niche film - related publications akin to ours, such as Cinefantastique and Starlog, only kept the presses running consistently for 36 and 33 years, respectively. Even the big - money, star - filled Premiere lasted a mere 23 years. So forgive us for being quite proud of our longevity. None of us could have foreseen, when Cinefex 1 was published in 1980, that we would still be here 40 years later — nor could we have predicted how the art, science and industry of visual effects would evolve. Oh, the wonders we have seen! We take great pride in having covered them, on the ground, as they occurred. We were there just as motion control technology was allowing camera moves in heretofore static visual effects shots, when optical gave way to digital compositing, when motion capture enabled the recording and transfer of movement onto animated characters. We were there when actors first had helmet - mounted cameras put on their heads. We were there when artists and technicians created an ambling stained
glass man and a liquid metal Terminator, when a watery pseudopod slithered into an oil rig on the ocean’s floor, when a brachiosaur munched leaves off a tree, when Gollum grasped his Precious, when the Polar Express rolled in, when a deep - space traveler piloted his ship into a black hole, when blue - skinned natives went to war with corporate miners, and when photoreal lions talked and sang their way across the African savannah. It was tempting to make our 40th anniversary issue all about that past — after all, reminiscing is what grand old ladies do. But we quickly discarded the idea as too pat, too self - indulgent, too expected. Instead of looking back, we decided to look forward. We would talk to visual effects supervisors and filmmakers, and though, inevitably, there would be some shuffling down Memory Lane, the focus of our discussions would be the future. What might the next 40 years bring? It was a big idea, but we think we’ve pulled it off. Inside this issue are exclusive interviews with directors James Cameron, George Lucas, Christopher Nolan and Robert Zemeckis, all of whom have delivered some of the most spectacular and innovative films covered in the pages of Cinefex. We also offer a roundtable discussion of visual effects past, present and future with 21 Academy Award - winning visual effects supervisors. We round out the issue with detailed coverage of The Mandalorian and Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker, which seemed fitting for an anniversary issue of Cinefex. By launching a new era in visual effects, the original Star Wars was, in part, responsible for the magazine’s inception, and The Empire Strikes Back graced the covers of both our second and third issues.
